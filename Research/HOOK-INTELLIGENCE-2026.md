# Hook Intelligence — what's working right now (July 2026)

*Compiled 2026-07-25 from ~22 live web searches across hook studies, carousel-algorithm
analyses, food-niche trend reporting, and app-marketing case studies. This is the
hook/format layer of the content engine: `Content-Engine/MASTER-PROMPT-V5.md` and the
weekly generator draw their hook patterns from here. Companion docs:
`Research/TARGET-USER-PROFILES.md` (who we're talking to),
`Research/CAROUSEL-MARKETING-PLAYBOOK.md` (the base format system).*

**Source-quality caveat, up front.** Most quantitative claims here (reach multipliers,
engagement percentages) come from marketing/SEO blogs and creator-tool companies, not
platform-published data. They agree with each other directionally (carousels > video,
swipe-completion is the ranking signal, 5–8 slides optimal), but treat exact numbers as
soft. Platform-official facts (TikTok's July 2026 AI-spam crackdown, Meta's Feb 2026 AI
labeling, Mosseri's carousel-reach statement) are firmer. **And the standing repo rule
applies: none of this replaces our own numbers. `Analytics/performance-log.jsonl` is the
only source of truth about what works for *us*, and it is still empty.** This doc is the
best available prior, not a result.

## The 2026 meta-structure

From an analysis of ~4,000 TikTok/IG videos (thecontentlabs): the single most common
structure in viral clips is **authority signal → curiosity gap → promise of payoff**.
The three most consistently viral hook families (vexub, 25-formula analysis):
**Contrarian Claim, Mistake Warning, List Tease.**

- 63% of highest-CTR videos hook within 3 seconds; the average viewing decision takes
  **1.7 seconds** (TikTok for Business data via OpusClip).
- For carousels, "the hook" = slide 1's headline. It must do the whole job.
- **Result-first beats setup-first**: "Product or Outcome Showcase" was the highest-
  performing hook type in the 4,000-video study (~2× the weakest type). Show the shocking
  number on slide 1; explain after.

## The hook library (20 formulas, each with a BiteBuddy adaptation)

### A. Mistake / correction family

| # | Formula | Template | BiteBuddy version |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mistake Warning** | "The #1 mistake people make when [goal]" / "3 mistakes keeping you [stuck]" | "3 'healthy' breakfast mistakes adding 400 calories before 9am" |
| 2 | **Direct Command** | "Stop [common behavior] — seriously, just stop" | "Stop guessing your calories. You're off by 40% (here's proof)" |
| 3 | **Doing-it-right-wrong** | "Your [effort] isn't working because you're [X] wrong. Not too little — wrong." | "You're not overeating. You're under-counting. There's a difference." |

Why they work: loss aversion + the anxiety that *you're* doing #4 wrong forces the swipe.
This family was already our F1 workhorse; still validated in 2026.

### B. Contrarian / myth family

| # | Formula | Template | BiteBuddy version |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Contrarian Claim** | "[Beloved thing] is the worst [category] for [goal]. Do this instead." | "Salads are the worst 'diet food.' Here are 5 that beat a Big Mac in calories." |
| 5 | **Authority Betrayal** | "What [experts/companies] won't tell you about [X]" | "What the menu won't tell you: the real calories in 7 'light' restaurant meals" |
| 6 | **My-Expert-Told-Me** | "My [dietitian] told me something that changed how I eat" | "A dietitian looked at my 'healthy' grocery haul. She flagged 6 items." |

⚠️ **Generic myth-busting has collapsed** — from 2023–24 dominance to ~8K average views
in the thecontentlabs dataset. Contrarian framing still works when it's **specific and
visual** (a real meal, a real number). "5 nutrition myths debunked" as a format is burned.

### C. Curiosity-gap / reveal family (native to carousels — the gap IS the swipe)

| # | Formula | Template | BiteBuddy version |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | **Guess the Calories** | Food photo + "Guess the calories. 99% get it wrong." Reveal 2 slides later. | **Our single best-fit format** — see below |
| 8 | **"Then I tracked it"** | "I thought I was eating healthy… then I tracked it and realized why I was stuck." | "I ate 'clean' for 30 days and gained weight. Then I scanned everything. Slide 4 hurt." |
| 9 | **Specific-Slide Tease** | "…Slide 5 will make you angry" / "#3 is the one nobody expects" | "7 foods with more sugar than a donut. #4 is marketed to kids." |
| 10 | **Outcome Showcase** | Show the shocking result FIRST, story after. | Slide 1 = scan result "1,240 calories" over an innocent-looking smoothie bowl |

Live examples found: @stanbrowneeey "Guess The Calories, Win $100"; @100caloriesclub;
an adjacent text-overlay quiz format at 11.3M views. IG re-serves carousels (a second
impression wave 24–48h later) when users reach slide 3+, so a tease that pulls people to
slide 3 has an outsized reach effect — **but the payoff must exist** (see anti-patterns).

**The #1 BiteBuddy format from this research:** guess-the-calories cover → "drop your
guess before you swipe" pinned comment → **the BiteBuddy scan screenshot as the
authoritative reveal slide.** It stacks the best-performing hook type (outcome showcase),
comment velocity (guesses), swipe-completion mechanics, and an organic product demo in
one post. The scan screenshot is *receipts*, not an ad.

(Panera reportedly underperforms on protein-per-dollar despite health-washed branding —
a ready-made contrarian post.)

## Carousel mechanics (2026 numbers)

### TikTok Photo Mode
- Carousels reported at **2–5× the reach of video** for the same accounts; a 698K-post
  analysis found **81% higher engagement and 82% more likes** than comparable videos.
  The Photo-Mode boost is still active in 2026, plus a dedicated photo-discovery feed.
- Ranking signals: **slide completion rate (primary)**, per-slide dwell, swipe-through
  rate, and **reverse swipes** (going back = strong interest). Carousels earn 25–60+
  seconds of dwell vs 5–15s for video.
- Slide count: sources converge on **5–8**.
- Format: 1080×1920 (9:16) native; caption carries a natural search keyword; save-prompt
  in caption; 5–8 hashtags on TikTok.

### Instagram
- **Mosseri (Feb 2026): carousels get ~1.4× the reach of static posts.** 2026 ranking
  shifted from likes to **total dwell time + completion rate**.
- **The slide-3 rule:** carousels get re-served in a second impression wave 24–48h later
  when users swipe to slide 3+ — the longest reach window of any feed format. Unfinished
  carousels get re-shown starting from a later slide.
- **<4 slides doesn't generate enough swipe data to trigger wider distribution.** 5–8
  optimal; >10 shows measurable completion drop.
- Format: 1080×1350 (4:5). End slide: save-worthy summary + CTA.

### YouTube Shorts
- YouTube now supports **image carousels up to 10 photos** with per-image text and music,
  pushed into the Shorts feed as first-class content in 2026. (Our ffmpeg slideshow-Short
  route also still works.)
- Shorts cold-seed pool is ~50–500 mostly-non-subscriber viewers; #1 metric is
  viewed-vs-swiped-away; ~70%+ retention for wider distribution. **Length math: a 15s
  Short needs ~100% retention to clear the watch-time gate; a 35s Short clears at ~65% —
  make slideshow Shorts 30–40s, not 15s.** (Our `build_youtube_short.py` timing should
  reflect this.)
- Titles: keyworded-but-human wins — "The real calories in a Chipotle bowl (I scanned
  it)" beats "calorie tracking app AI food scanner." Three hashtags. March 2026:
  **original audio now beats trending audio for accounts under 50K subs.**

### ⚠️ Cadence findings that override our current schedule assumptions
- **TikTok: 1–3 posts/day is the safe band; 4–5+/day triggers algorithm flags.** Our
  3/day is at the top of safe.
- **Instagram: multiple same-day feed posts compete with each other, weaken early
  signals, and pattern-match to spam. Recommended max ~1 feed post/day** (Buffer's 2M+
  post analysis: the performance jump is from 1–2 to 3–5 posts/*week*, hard diminishing
  returns after). Our 3/day cross-post to IG is aggressive-to-harmful, and the 22 July
  five-simultaneous-post incident is a textbook throttle trigger.
- IG now applies **duplicate-content penalties to identical cross-posted media** — strip
  watermarks, vary crops/captions between platforms.
- **Action for `carousel-publish`:** keep 3/day on TikTok; drop Instagram to 1–2/day,
  well-spaced, never simultaneous; make the rate limit CLAUDE.md asks for enforce
  per-platform spacing, not just batch spacing.

## Food-niche trends (mid-2026)

1. **Protein obsession is the defining lifestyle trend.** 52% of consumers try new
   high-protein foods because of TikTok/IG; 8-in-10 Americans prioritize protein daily;
   "protein-maxxing" is a named behavior. Protein-forward framing ("49g protein, 380
   cal") is the lingua franca of viral food content.
2. **Fibermaxxing is the breakout 2026 counter-trend** — Gen-Z-driven, +21% social
   conversation, named by Whole Foods and Johns Hopkins as a defining 2026 trend.
   **"Fiber is the macro nobody tracks" is early and uncrowded** — and BiteBuddy showing
   fiber per scan is a differentiator vs pure calorie apps. (Use the behavior, not the
   word — "-maxxing" vocabulary is nearing its cringe threshold per the May 2026 Salon
   backlash piece.)
3. **The GLP-1 era is reshaping demand:** ~10M Americans on GLP-1s in 2026 (projected
   30M+ by 2030); ranked the #1 health trend of 2026. Users report 93% smaller portions
   and 38% more protein drinks. Content that serves them: protein-in-a-tiny-appetite,
   portion-size content, muscle-preservation-while-losing framing, small-but-nutrient-
   dense meals. **This audience needs effortless tracking and nobody in the carousel
   space owns them yet.**
4. **Restaurant/chain macro content is a winning sub-niche:** viral Chipotle order hacks,
   McDonald's high-protein swaps, chain-vs-chain matchups, 17–50-chain macro tier lists,
   protein-per-dollar rankings.
5. **WIEIAD ("what I eat in a day") carries active ED-adjacent criticism** from clinical
   voices in 2026. If ever used: "what I *scanned* in a day — including the surprises,"
   non-restrictive framing, never a low daily total as the point.

## App-install conversion (reach ≠ installs)

Case studies: Cal AI (~$8M ARR; volume micro-influencer outreach + founder demo TikToks +
multi-account posting; the scan "magic moment" IS the creative) and Rizz (550M views
across 15+ faceless accounts, ~450K avg views/video; many accounts, one repeatable
blueprint, slight audience variation per account — never bet one account).

What converts, from slideshow-app-marketing sources:

- Working structure: hook slide → 5–7 value slides (one point each) → **CTA slide**:
  "the app is called [name]" name-drops work because TikTok users search the App Store
  directly. Our exact line stays `Search 'BiteBuddy: Ai calorie scanner'`.
- **Engagement CTAs ("comment X") maximize distribution but can reduce link clicks.
  Conversion CTAs (bio link, app-name drop) drive installs.** Best practice: comment-bait
  CTA on discovery/reach posts, hard install CTA on demo/proof posts — and measure the
  two separately.
- Volume-first testing: winning app brands ship 10–20 slideshow *variations* weekly and
  read the data — which is exactly what `carousel-optimize` is built for, once the
  measurement gap is closed.
- Highest-converting organic angles for utility apps: problem→solution demo ("I thought
  I was eating healthy, then I tracked it"), testimonial framing, and soft-sell inside
  genuinely useful content (cheat-sheet post whose last slide says "I pulled these
  numbers with BiteBuddy").
- Hook-rate target: >25% 3-second holds; 90% of recall lands in the first 6 seconds.

## Anti-patterns — what's dead, burned, or dangerous in 2026

1. **Engagement bait is formally policed.** TikTok's Integrity & Authenticity policy
   flags "wait for it" / "link in bio for the secret" with no immediate value as
   low-quality bait → reach suppression. Curiosity gaps must pay off. A slide tease
   ("#4 will surprise you") is fine only when #4 delivers.
2. **Generic myth-busting is burned** (~8K avg views). Specific + visual contrarian
   claims only.
3. **The AI-slop crackdown is live, account-level, and names health content as a
   priority category (TikTok, July 2026).** TikTok removed 86M fake accounts in Q1 2026
   and is targeting *accounts dedicated to AI-generated spam*; it joined the C2PA
   steering committee. Meta unified AI labeling across IG/FB (Feb 2026) and auto-flags
   via C2PA metadata — a file flagged on one platform gets flagged on the next. Labeled
   AI content is only mildly penalized; **unlabeled** AI content risks auto-labeling,
   suppression, or removal.
   **This describes us uncomfortably well:** a faceless health account posting 3×/day of
   AI-generated slides. Mitigations, in priority order: real food photography and real
   scan screenshots wherever possible (receipts > renders); disclose AI where required;
   keep design looking human-crafted and vary templates; keep the account visibly
   human-operated (replies, pinned comments, occasional native content).
4. **Mass/simultaneous posting** — covered in cadence above; bursty posting (10 one day,
   0 the next) is worse than steady.
5. **Burned aesthetics:** polished generic brand content, hashtag stuffing (3–8 targeted
   tags only), keyword-stuffed Shorts titles, and the generic template-y "Canva
   infographic" look that now reads as AI slop — viral-format copycatting in this niche
   jumped from ~8 to 11+ imitation posts/day between April and May 2026, so template
   variation is a survival trait, not polish.
6. **WIEIAD with restrictive framing** — reputational risk; our framing stays "know your
   food," never "eat less."

## Strategic reads for BiteBuddy (summary)

1. Lead format: **guess-the-calories with the scan screenshot as the reveal** — hook,
   comments, completion, and product demo in one.
2. Use the scan screenshot as **receipts inside content**, not only as a final CTA slide.
3. Own the uncrowded angles: **GLP-1-companion content, fiber-per-scan, protein-per-
   dollar chain rankings.**
4. Fix cadence per-platform: TikTok 3/day OK, **IG 1–2/day max, never simultaneous**,
   vary media between platforms.
5. Ship hook *variations* of winners weekly (10–20 variants is the app-marketing norm) —
   which requires the analytics loop to finally have data. Measurement first, still.
