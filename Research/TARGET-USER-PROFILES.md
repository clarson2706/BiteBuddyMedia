# Target User Profiles — who actually downloads BiteBuddy

*Compiled 2026-07-25 from ~26 live web searches: market data, Cal AI case studies and
its April 2026 App Store removal, academic literature on tracking abandonment, Gallup/KFF
GLP-1 polling, and user-complaint sources. **Evidence caveat:** Reddit blocks automated
crawling, so subreddit sentiment comes via secondary sources that quote those
communities. Quotes marked verbatim are verbatim from the cited secondary source. The
postpartum and midlife profiles have the thinnest voice-of-customer evidence — worth
Connor spending 30 minutes in r/Mommit, r/GLP1, and competitor App Store reviews before
treating those two as settled.*

**How to use this doc:** every piece of content the engine produces names one primary
persona from this list. `Content-Engine/MASTER-PROMPT-V5.md` enforces that via its
`Audience` column; hook selection per persona comes from
`Research/HOOK-INTELLIGENCE-2026.md`.

## Market baseline (who tracks food at all)

- ~192M active users of nutrition-tracking apps worldwide, growing ~13–15% CAGR.
- MyFitnessPal (200M+ registered): ~55% female / 45% male; **44% aged 25–34**, 21% aged
  18–24 — two-thirds of the biggest tracker's users are 18–34.
- Gallup: 55% of US adults want to lose weight, only ~27% are actively working on it —
  a huge "intending but not acting" pool that download-trigger moments convert.
- The January spike is real: fitness app installs +36% MoM in January. New Year's is the
  single biggest predictable trigger moment in the category. (Plan Q4 content around it.)
- **Cal AI proved the photo-scan market** (15M+ downloads, ~$30M in 2025, sold to
  MyFitnessPal) — and then handed challengers an opening: **Apple removed Cal AI from
  the App Store in April 2026 for deceptive billing design**, with "scam" recurring in
  reviews. A trustworthy, friendly, transparent-billing alternative is a real position,
  not a platitude.

## The eight personas

Priority tiers given zero current users + our channels (TikTok/IG/Shorts) + Buddy's
kind tone: **Tier 1 = P1 Serial Restarter, P2 Ozempic Optimizer** (underserved by
Cal AI's bro-coded marketing, best tone fit). **Tier 2 = P3 Bulker** (proven demand,
biggest content channel), **P4 Zero-Friction Professional** (best Pro-conversion
economics), **P5 Glow-Up Tracker** (huge but ethically loaded). **Tier 3 = P6 Wake-Up
Call, P7 Reclaimer, P8 Dining-Hall Navigator.**

### P1 — The Serial Restarter ("here we go again")
- **Who:** 28–50, any gender (slight female skew), has already used MyFitnessPal /
  Lose It / Noom and quit. A *switcher*, not a first-timer.
- **Evidence:** 84% of adults have attempted weight loss; ~80% of diets fail; the
  January +36% install spike is this persona en masse.
- **Goal:** Lose the same 20–40 lbs again — sustainably this time.
- **Emotional driver:** Hope mixed with self-distrust. "When you start and fail over and
  over again, you lose confidence in yourself." The all-or-nothing trap: one slip →
  "I've failed" → delete app.
- **Why they quit last time (the research is unambiguous):** logging took too long (the
  #1 stated quit reason across the academic literature), databases full of wrong
  entries, one missed day killed the streak, the log became "a log of excesses."
- **Download trigger:** Monday. January 1. A post-holiday photo. A doctor's comment.
  Clothes not fitting. An emotional micro-event — they download within minutes of it.
- **The pitch that lands:** *photo scanning removes the exact friction that made you
  quit last time.* This was Cal AI's core message and it demonstrably converts.
- **Content that lands:** "why your tracking never sticks" explainers, realistic
  ("it took 18 months") framing, streak-repair/no-guilt features, "the app that doesn't
  shame you on day 4."
- **Hooks:** Mistake Warning, Doing-it-right-wrong ("You're not overeating. You're
  under-counting."), Habit Attribution, "Then I tracked it."
- **Avoid:** hype, perfection framing, broken-streak shame. Streaks are a double-edged
  sword here — market the streak *repair*, not just the streak.
- **Platform:** Instagram + TikTok; older end on Facebook.

### P2 — The Ozempic Optimizer (GLP-1 user)
- **Who:** Skews 40–64, female-leaning, middle-income; includes compounded-semaglutide
  users. **11% of US adults currently take a GLP-1 for weight loss (Gallup, July 2026;
  up from 8% in 2025)**; 1 in 8 taking one for any reason (KFF). Fastest-growing
  segment in the category; a whole app sub-industry (Pep, MeAgain, MyFitnessPal's
  dedicated GLP-1 mode) appeared in 18 months.
- **Goal:** NOT calorie restriction — the drug does that. They need to **hit 80–100g
  protein and enough fiber on a suppressed appetite to avoid losing muscle** (clinical
  guidance: 20–40% of GLP-1 weight loss can be lean mass without enough protein;
  providers explicitly tell patients to track protein with an app).
- **Emotional driver:** Freedom + fear. The drug quieted "food noise" (their word — "a
  constant nagging to eat something"); they're terrified of wasting an expensive,
  possibly temporary window, of "Ozempic face"/muscle loss, and of regain. Half say the
  drugs are hard to afford — they are financially invested in making the window count.
- **Pain points:** Every classic tracker assumes you're fighting appetite; they need the
  opposite question ("did I eat *enough* protein?"). Tiny odd meals are annoying to log
  manually. Nausea days break streaks.
- **Download trigger:** First injection week; prescriber/dietitian says "track your
  protein"; a scary muscle-loss video.
- **Content that lands:** "80g of protein on a tiny appetite," small-but-nutrient-dense
  meals, protein-first chain orders, zero judgment, side-effect-day grace. They live on
  TikTok and Facebook groups.
- **Hooks:** Demographic Call-Out (without naming medications — "if your appetite is
  smaller than your protein goal…"), Cheat Sheet, Specific-Number Promise (protein
  versions).
- **Sensitivities:** many are private about the medication — serve the *situation*
  (small appetite, protein goal), don't diagnose the viewer. No medical claims, ever
  (guardrails). **Nobody in the carousel space owns this audience yet.**

### P3 — The Bulker (new gym-goer, protein-focused young man)
- **Who:** Male, 16–27, student or early-career, 0–2 years lifting. 47% of new 2025 gym
  joins were Gen Z; 18–24 has the highest US gym-membership rate (35.5%). This was
  **Cal AI's founding user** (founder started lifting to gain weight) — the proven
  photo-scan beachhead.
- **Goal:** Gain muscle; hit a daily protein number. Calories are secondary to protein
  grams.
- **Emotional driver:** Visible physique change; optimization identity — tracking is
  part of the aesthetic, not a chore to hide.
- **Pain points:** Weighing chicken and rice is tedious; MFP database junk; "hardgainer"
  anxiety (am I eating enough?).
- **Download trigger:** Started lifting 2–8 weeks ago; a creator or friend said "you're
  not growing because you're not eating enough protein — track it."
- **Content that lands:** protein-per-dollar breakdowns, chain protein rankings,
  scan-my-bulk-meal, anabolic-recipe genre, streak flexing, memes.
- **Hooks:** List Tease, Best Order At [Chain], Contrarian Claim, Outcome Showcase.
- **Buddy risk:** could read as childish to this segment **unless played for memes** —
  self-aware Buddy works, earnest Buddy doesn't.
- **Platform:** TikTok first, YouTube Shorts second (lifting over-indexes on YT).

### P4 — The Zero-Friction Professional
- **Who:** 27–45, office/hybrid, eats out or orders in heavily, disposable income —
  **best Pro-conversion economics of any segment.**
- **Evidence:** time burden is the #1 quit reason in the literature; ~1/3 of non-users
  say diet apps are too time-consuming; adults underestimate restaurant meals by ~20%;
  92% of restaurant meals exceed recommended per-meal calories; 21% of US adult
  calories come from restaurants. Photo-scan is the only logging method that fits their
  actual diet.
- **Goal:** Stop the slow creep (5 lbs/year); feel in control without tracking becoming
  a hobby.
- **Emotional driver:** Efficiency identity (Whoop/Oura crowd) — food is the
  unoptimized blind spot. Mild DoorDash guilt.
- **Download trigger:** Annual-physical numbers; clothes fit change; a colleague
  scanning lunch.
- **Content that lands:** "the healthiest order at [chain]," desk-lunch upgrades,
  30-second efficiency content, restaurant-calorie reality checks.
- **Hooks:** Best Order At [Chain], Authority Betrayal ("what the menu won't tell
  you"), Same-Calories Side-by-Side, Cheat Sheet.
- **Sensitivities:** analytical — don't overclaim AI precision (see accuracy framing
  below); subscription fatigue (transparent pricing is a feature); Buddy must be quiet/
  optional in content aimed here.
- **Platform:** YouTube Shorts + Instagram.
- Note: the "food-ordering optimizer" idea was **refuted as a standalone persona** —
  it's this persona's defining behavior, so it lives here as content angles.

### P5 — The Glow-Up Tracker (aesthetic-driven young woman)
- **Who:** Female, 18–30, urban/college, IG+TikTok native. The core of MFP's dominant
  25–34 bracket; #WhatIEatInADay has 9.8B+ views.
- **Goal:** Lose 5–20 lbs / "tone up" for an event or season; increasingly
  protein-aware (the "toned," not "skinny," era).
- **Emotional driver:** Comparison and control.
- **⚠️ This is the segment where tracking most often turns dark.** 73% of ED-recovery
  respondents in one study believed MFP contributed to their eating disorder. Verbatim
  (therapist.com): *"I went from casually logging meals… to obsessively tracking every
  morsel… then restricting myself further when I didn't see results fast enough."*
  SkinnyTok was banned by TikTok mid-2025 and promptly rebranded ("balance," "gut
  health," "glow-up") — demand intensity plus platform scrutiny.
- **What this means for us:** gentle, anti-shame positioning is simultaneously the
  ethical requirement, the platform-safety requirement, and the differentiation. "No
  red numbers, no guilt." Never restriction framing, never low-total WIEIAD content.
- **Download trigger:** Event on the calendar (vacation/wedding, April–May "summer"
  spike), a creator's transformation, New Year.
- **Content that lands:** pretty food content, high-protein "girl dinner," swaps that
  keep the craving, aesthetic app screenshots. Buddy is an asset here (cute =
  shareable).
- **Hooks:** Same-Calories Side-by-Side, Guess the Calories, Smart Swaps, POV Mirror.

### P6 — The Wake-Up Call (midlife health scare)
- **Who:** 45–65, male-skewing, less app-fluent, high willingness to pay. 96M US adults
  have prediabetes (most undiagnosed).
- **Goal:** Get A1c/cholesterol/BP down; avoid medication; "be around for the
  grandkids."
- **Emotional driver:** Fear converted to resolve. The trigger is a literal sentence
  from a doctor: "your A1c puts you in the prediabetic range" — often followed by
  "keep a food diary," with no tooling.
- **Pain points:** overwhelmed by nutrition complexity; fiddly search UIs; embarrassed
  to be a beginner.
- **Content that lands:** simple swaps, "what your labs mean" adjacent content (careful:
  educational, never diagnostic), sodium/sugar reality checks. **BiteBuddy's weekly
  report framed as "something to show your doctor" is a genuine hook.**
- **Platform reality check:** they're on Facebook and long-form YouTube, not TikTok —
  the weakest fit for our current channels. YouTube Shorts is the reachable surface.
  Treat as a secondary audience for Shorts + Facebook until channels expand.
- **Hooks:** My-Expert-Told-Me, Guess the Sodium/Sugar, Mistake Warning.

### P7 — The Reclaimer (postpartum)
- **Who:** Female, 25–40, 3–18 months postpartum, time-starved, phone always in hand
  (night feeds = heavy scroll hours). ~75% of women are heavier at 1 year postpartum
  than pre-pregnancy; 24% by >20 lbs.
- **Goal:** Lose baby weight **without compromising milk supply or energy.**
  Breastfeeding adds ~340–500 kcal/day of need and generic apps mis-set targets — a
  real product gap.
- **Emotional driver:** Reclaiming identity after a body that "belonged to someone
  else"; guilt about self-focus; bounce-back-culture resentment.
- **Pain points:** zero hands, zero time (one-handed photo logging is a literal selling
  point); untracked toddler leftovers; sleep-deprivation snacking.
- **Download trigger:** cleared for exercise at the 6-week checkup; weaning; a
  mom-creator's honest postpartum content.
- **Content that lands:** "5-minute high-protein snacks while holding a baby,"
  anti-bounce-back honesty, eat-more-not-less framing (supply anxiety).
- **Sensitivities:** extreme sensitivity to diet-culture pressure — anything shame-y is
  disqualifying. Evidence here is the thinnest of the eight; validate before leaning in.
- **Platform:** Instagram first, TikTok second.

### P8 — The Dining-Hall Navigator (college variant)
- **Who:** 18–22, TikTok-native, broke. Freshman weight gain is real but modest (~7
  lbs). Dining halls have no labels or portions — photo-scan is uniquely useful.
- **Verdict from research:** real, but mostly a **younger variant of P3 and P5** — use
  as an age-band angle (dining-hall content, budget protein, semester-start timing),
  not a standalone persona. Price-sensitive: free tier matters.
- **⚠️** University calorie-tracker users show elevated disordered-eating attitudes in
  several studies — the P5 sensitivities apply at full strength.

## The anti-persona: The Data-Nerd Defector

The MacroFactor/Cronometer audience — evidence-based lifters and analytically minded
dieters who weigh food to the gram and left MFP over its junk database and paywalls.
**Do not market to them.** They are loudly, publicly skeptical of AI photo estimation
and will generate exactly the "AI was off by 40%" stitch-tests that poison social
proof. Never publish accuracy-first claims that invite their tests. Their critiques
still matter — they define the accuracy-framing rules below.

## Cross-cutting findings (bake into every post)

**Why people quit tracking, ranked by evidence weight:**
1. **Time/effort burden** — #1 across the literature. The entire strategic basis for
   photo scanning; Cal AI's revenue validates it.
2. **Database frustration** — wrong entries destroy trust in the numbers.
3. **Shame spiral** — UCL/Loughborough 2025: apps "inadvertently promote feelings of
   shame, disappointment, and demotivation"; red numbers → guilt → users skip logging
   shameful foods → corrupted log → quit. Guilt drives the "f-it binge."
4. **All-or-nothing collapse** — one missed day → "I failed" → delete.
5. **Obsession fear** — a meaningful minority quit deliberately because tracking became
   compulsive. Gentle accountability is both safer and differentiating.
6. **Paywall betrayal** — MFP's barcode-scanner paywall is the canonical grievance;
   post-Cal-AI-takedown, "no tricks" pricing is a legitimate marketing angle.

**AI-accuracy skepticism — the #1 comment-section objection we will face.**
Documented, circulating Cal AI failures: an apple identified as tikka masala; a
tofu/feta/olive-oil salad estimated at 450 kcal vs a realistic 800–900 (hidden oils are
the systematic blind spot); accuracy "drops sharply" on large portions (2025 study).
**Framing rule: never claim precision; claim consistency and sufficiency** — "close
enough, every meal, beats perfect twice a week." Show the estimate being *edited* —
transparency converts skeptics better than accuracy claims. (This also matches the
app's real behavior: estimates are user-reviewed.)

**Gamification:** streaks work (Duolingo: 7-day streak → 2.3× daily engagement) but a
broken streak is the Restarter's known quit trigger — streak repair should be
*marketed*, not hidden.

**The Cal AI conversion playbook, distilled:** ride existing viral formats → show the
scan as a natural in-video beat, not a pitch → payoff at the end. Volume via many small
creators beat big one-off sponsorships. Their audience: young users "who grew up with
TikTok and expect apps to be effortless."
