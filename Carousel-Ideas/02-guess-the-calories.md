# F2 — "Guess the Calories"

> **⚠️ UPDATED CONTENT MODEL (2026-07) — this overrides the slide plan below.**
> Posts now **educate first and sell last**: body slides teach (nutrition facts,
> real food photos, honest tips) with **no app UI and no per-slide Buddy branding**.
> The **only app slide is the last one** — the Today-home hero in a phone silhouette
> + "Download BiteBuddy — free on the App Store" + a topic call-to-action. Every
> image is a constant **1080×1350 (4:5)**. Use the angle bank + hooks below for
> ideas, but build slides per the current SKILL.md "Content model", not the old
> screenshot-heavy tables here.


**Format:** Interactive reveal carousel. A food photo on one slide, the calorie
count revealed on the next, Buddy reacting. Repeat, then CTA.
**Hook type:** Curiosity gap (the strongest driver of swipe-through completion —
the ranking signal).
**Why it works:** "Guess the calories" is a *native, proven* food-TikTok format
with huge saves and comments (people guess in the comments — free engagement that
feeds reach). It's genuinely useful, so it earns the final product slide instead
of feeling like an ad. Highest save-rate format of the four.

## The fill-in-the-blank

```
COVER:  "Guess the calories 👀 (most people are way off)"   buddy_thinking + food
Qn:     big appetizing food photo + "How many? 🍔"           (no answer yet)
An:     same food + big "≈ ___ cal" + a reacting Buddy pose  (payoff)
CTA:    "Stop guessing — BiteBuddy snaps it for you."        phone frame + buddy_happy
```

Alternating Q/A is what drives swipe-through — never put the answer on the same
slide as the question.

**Theme variations (endless):**
- "Guess the calories: Starbucks edition ☕"
- "Guess the calories: 'healthy' snacks that aren't"
- "Guess the protein 💪 (this one's a series)"
- "Cheaper vs pricier — which meal has more calories?"
- "Restaurant vs homemade: same dish, wildly different numbers"

## Guardrails
Use **realistic ballpark** numbers and label them "≈" — they're illustrative,
not a claim. Don't moralize foods; keep it playful ("sugar in a trench coat," not
"this is bad for you"). App numbers shown are AI estimates the user reviews.
